# The Neighbours

Joplin sits among note tools that
make different bargains. These are
characterisations from the projects'
own documentation, not benchmarks;
the lab ran Joplin only. Where a
neighbour has its own guide in this
series, it is named once — this
chapter still completes without it.

## Obsidian: files first, app second

[Obsidian for Mere Mortals](https://paulsullivan33-dev.github.io/obsidian-for-mere-mortals/)
starts from the opposite end: a
*vault* of plain Markdown files in an
ordinary folder, which the app
decorates with links, backlinks and
a graph. There is no database of
record — the files are the thing —
and sync is your folder's problem
(their paid sync, a synced drive, a
git repository). Choose Obsidian
when the files themselves are the
point and linking is how you think;
choose Joplin when you want the app
to *own* the filing — sync,
encryption, attachments — and are
content that the folder underneath
is Joplin-shaped.

## Plain files and a text editor

The oldest neighbour needs no
install: a folder of `.md` files and
whatever editor you already trust.
It wins on simplicity and loses on
everything this guide demonstrated —
no tags across files, no attachments
with bookkeeping, no sync answer, no
encryption answer, search by `grep`.
Many Joplin users started here and
moved when the folder outgrew the
method; Joplin's Markdown export
(chapter 9) keeps the way back
open, which is more than most apps
offer.

## BookStack: notes for a group, on a server

[BookStack for Mere Mortals](https://paulsullivan33-dev.github.io/bookstack-for-mere-mortals/)
is a wiki: shelves and books on a
server you host, accounts and
permissions, pages written for other
people to read. If the audience is a
club or a household and the shape is
"the manual we all share", a wiki
fits better than any personal note
app. Joplin is the notebook in your
own pocket; BookStack is the ring
binder on the shared shelf.

## The account-based apps

Evernote, Apple Notes, OneNote and
their kin make the opposite opening
bargain from Joplin: hand the company
your notes and in return get polish,
effortless sync, and handwriting and
clipping tricks an open project
matches slowly or not at all. The
cost is the one chapter 1 named —
export formats that fight back,
terms that change, a subscription
between you and your own words.
Chapter 9's import path exists
because so many people make this
move, usually in one direction.
